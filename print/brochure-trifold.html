01 · The problem

Stroke takes away movement.
Therapy is hard to reach.

Hemiparesis — weakness on one side of the body — is the most common motor effect of stroke. Recovery needs hundreds of guided repetitions, but therapists, clinic time and travel are limited, especially once patients go home.

Our answer

A lightweight upper-limb exoskeleton that follows the patient's intention, gives only the help that is missing, and keeps the care team informed — at home.

03 · AI adaptive assistance
τmotor = (1 − ê) · τtaskê = patient effort estimated from sEMG + joint motion

As the patient gets stronger, motor help fades automatically. Fatigue or spasm raises assistance or triggers a soft stop.

Safety first

Mechanical joint limits, spasm detection and an instant soft-stop button protect the patient.

Upper-limb focus

Elbow flexion–extension with a tendon-assisted glove for grip practice.
